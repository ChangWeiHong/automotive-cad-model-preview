Sun angle

Latitude, date, clock time and timezone set the sun's height. A low sun crosses up to 38× more air, so blue scatters out and direct light turns gold, then red.

Air molecules

Rayleigh scattering grows as 1/λ⁴: blue sky, warmer sun. High altitude (Denver, La Paz) means less air, a whiter sun and a deeper blue sky.

Aerosols and humidity

Dust, smog, smoke and sea salt scatter all colours more evenly, so skies go milky and shadows soften. Humid air swells these particles and adds haze.

Temperature

Warm air holds more water vapour. That adds haze and absorption bands near 690 and 720 nm. Hot clear days also add near-ground heat haze.

Ozone and clouds

Ozone absorbs orange-yellow near 600 nm, which keeps twilight blue. Clouds flatten the spectrum into cool, even light with no hard shadows.

Street light

Sodium lamps emit a narrow band near 589 nm, so reds, blues and greens collapse toward brown-grey. LED lamps keep most of the paint's hue.
